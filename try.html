Five things to try

Each one is a gesture you would make on stage.

Press PLAY
A one-bar count-in shows on screen, then the first song starts, with sound. STOP stops it.
Jump to a song
Tap Gravity in the list, whether the set is playing or stopped. The set jumps there.
Loop a section
Open the Live view, the waveform icon at the top. Tap the loop icon of a section: once, twice, endless, then release. Tap a section further down and its loop is prepared: it starts when the song gets there. The voice announces the next section a few bars ahead and says where the loop stands: Loop, One more time, Last time.
Chain two songs
A song normally stops at its end. Tap the padlock at the top right to edit the list, then tap the arrow of a song to play straight into the next one.
Mute a track
Open the menu at the top right, then Ableton Tracks. Mute or solo a track while the song plays: you hear it drop out, and the main level at the top follows.

What is simulated, what is real

In this demo

  • A small set of three songs, simulated in your browser tab.
  • No Ableton: your browser produces the sound of the three songs, one track per instrument, and plays the voice announcements.
  • Nothing is sent or saved. Close the tab and it is gone.
  • Features reserved for higher plans in the app, such as section loops and cue, are all open here.

In Markerlist

  • Your own Ableton Live set, read from your locators and sections. Your project is never modified.
  • Windows or Mac, with every edition of Ableton Live 11 and 12.
  • A phone, a tablet, a computer or a MIDI pedal, on your local Wi-Fi.
  • A free trial with no account and no credit card.
